# A landing page argues from the reader's problem to the purchase

## Rule

A landing page is one argument, in the order a buyer thinks it through: the problem
they have today, why the usual workarounds fail, what changes with the product, how
it works in a few steps, proof that it is real, the objections a buyer raises
(security, effort, cost), the price, and one call to action. Each section answers the
question the section before it raised.

Write it in simple, spoken language, with examples from the reader's own day. Show
how little effort setup takes, with the real steps, because effort is a buying
objection.

## Why

On 2026-10-16 the founder judged the round-1 landing structure to be "like 10% of what
needs to be done there, content must be 100x better, use simple language, humanized
and arranged in a way that it leads reader from problem statement to willing to buy
this product." The one part he praised was the setup section ("Set up in about five
minutes.") because it shows how easy onboarding is.

## How to apply

- Outline the page as the reader's questions before writing any copy.
- Read only the headlines, in order. They should tell the whole story.
- Keep the promise from [bounded autonomy leads the positioning](content-position-bounded-autonomy.md):
  the problem is that AI cannot safely work on production today, and the answer is
  the work it can now do on its own.
- Cut any section that does not move the reader one step closer to trying the product.
